Capture ratio by region

Wind’s earnings per MWh divided by the region’s time-weighted average price. Below the 1.0 line, wind is worth less than the average megawatt hour — the cannibalisation discount a merchant project is exposed to.

What wind earns as wind penetration rises

Output-weighted price in each band of wind as a share of operational demand. Read it against the solar chart. The lowest wind band is not the cheapest wind band — it is the middle of the day, when solar sets the price. Compare bands of similar solar.

Solar in the same bands

The control variable. Where this line is high the price says more about solar than about wind, which is why the band order alone cannot be read as a cannibalisation curve.

Penetration bands in full

Median penetration per band, what the region paid, what wind earned, and how much of the fleet’s output landed there.

How concentrated is the revenue?

Share of regional wind revenue earned in the best-paying intervals, ranked by the revenue each one produced. Two regions can share a capture price and carry very different risk: revenue that arrives in a handful of intervals depends on scarcity events surviving.

How long does a negative price last?

Share of each region’s time below zero spent inside runs of a given length. Share alone cannot separate a rounding error from a curtailment policy; duration can.

Run lengths in full

Note the two percentage columns together: a bucket holding few runs but most of the intervals is the whole point of the measure.

Do these farms generate at the same time?

Mean correlation between two wind farms’ five-minute capacity factors. The diagonal is two farms inside one region; off the diagonal, one in each.

Correlation detail

Portfolio correlation is the two regions’ aggregate output moving together, which runs stronger than the typical farm pair because averaging within a region cancels the farm-level noise and leaves the shared weather. The spread between the weakest and strongest pair is the reminder that a regional mean is not a siting decision.

Does the wind arrive when demand does?

Wind output as a share of operational demand, by hour of the NEM day, averaged over every day held.

Shape detail

The ratio and the two means behind it. Demand is operational, so it is already net of rooftop solar — the middle of the day looks smaller here than a gross demand curve would.

Wind that was available and did not run

The gap between AEMO’s unconstrained availability forecast for semi-scheduled wind and what was actually metered, split by whether the price was negative at the time.

Curtailment detail

Availability covers semi-scheduled units only, so output is filtered to match — 27 of 109 registered wind DUIDs are not semi-scheduled, and comparing the two populations put Tasmania’s output above its own forecast. Gaps inside the tolerance are counted as forecast error rather than curtailment.

Revenue and capture detail

Capture price is what wind earned per MWh; TWAP is what an even generator would have earned. Below 1.0 is the cannibalisation effect.
